% ============================ ABSTRACT ===============================
\setcounter{page}{1}
\begin{center}\textbf{Abstract}\end{center}
\vspace{-4pt}
\begin{quote}\small
Weekly forecasts of rainfall and temperature two to six weeks ahead are useful for agriculture, water management and disaster
planning in India, but it is not clear which forecast system works best over the country. In the first part of this thesis we compared
nine physics-based and AI-based forecast systems over India using the same dates, regions and scores (IndiaS2S-Bench). The AI model
FuXi-S2S gave the best monsoon rainfall forecasts at long lead times, but its ensemble spread was too small. We trained a small neural
network that corrects the FuXi-S2S ensemble, which reduced the rainfall CRPS by 16.2\%. Both studies have been accepted at the
\textit{Tackling Climate Change with Machine Learning} workshop at NeurIPS 2026. We also tested statistical methods for combining
several models, and set up an experimental forecast website (s2s.scdlds.com). In the remaining period we plan to use deep learning,
in particular generative models, to produce calibrated forecasts of rainfall and temperature together.
\end{quote}

% ============================ 1. INTRODUCTION ========================
\section{Introduction}
Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasts cover the period from about two weeks to two months ahead~\cite{vitart2017s2s,white2017apps}.
This range lies between weather forecasts and seasonal outlooks. It is important for India because most of the annual rainfall comes
during the southwest monsoon (June--September, JJAS), and a dry or wet spell of a few weeks can affect sowing, reservoir operation
and flood preparation~\cite{imd2022lpa}.

In the last few years several AI weather models have been released, for example Pangu, GraphCast, FuXi-S2S, NeuralGCM and
FourCastNet~3~\cite{bi2023pangu,lam2023graphcast,chen2024fuxis2s,kochkov2024neuralgcm,bonev2025fcn3}. Most of them are evaluated on
global averages~\cite{rasp2024wb2,nathaniel2024chaosbench}. A model that does well globally may not do well over India, and its skill
can also change with region and season~\cite{malik2024s2s}. Rainfall is the most difficult variable at these lead times, so we work
with weekly means and with anomalies from climatology, and we use climatology as the basic reference forecast.

The main questions of this thesis are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which forecast system works best over India for a given variable, lead time, region and season?
\item Can a small regional model correct the errors of a global AI forecast, in particular its ensemble spread?
\item Can deep learning models, in particular generative models, give reliable probabilistic forecasts of rainfall and temperature together?
\end{enumerate}

% ============================ 2. WORK DONE ===========================
\section{Work done so far}

\subsection{IndiaS2S-Bench: comparing forecast systems over India}
We collected forecasts from four operational centres (ECMWF, NCEP, UKMO and CMA) from the S2S database, and ran five AI and hybrid
models ourselves from ERA5 initial conditions (FuXi-S2S, NeuralGCM, FourCastNet~3, DLESyM-v0 and DLESyM-v1)~\cite{raj2026bench}.
All models were evaluated only on dates after their training period. We used 517 start dates from 2020 to 2024 (516 for
temperature) and converted forecast days 1--42 into six weekly means on a 1.5$^\circ$ grid. Rainfall was verified against IMERG and
against IMD gauge data~\cite{huffman2023imerg,pai2014imd}, and 2\,m temperature against ERA5~\cite{hersbach2020era5}. Scores were
computed for All-India, the four IMD homogeneous regions and four seasons.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/skill_summary.pdf}
\caption{(a, b) All-India anomaly correlation (ACC) by lead week for four of the systems in IndiaS2S-Bench~\cite{raj2026bench}. ECMWF
rainfall for Week~3 is missing in the archive. (c) Fraction of observations inside the 90\% range of the FuXi-S2S ensemble for
JJAS rainfall, before and after calibration~\cite{raj2026adapter} (2020--2024, 169 cases per lead).}
\label{fig:skill}

The main results are:
\begin{itemize}
\item The simple average of six models (MME) has the highest Week-1 ACC for rainfall (0.622) and temperature (0.596)
(Figure~\ref{fig:skill}a,b), and the lowest RMSE in 11 of 12 target--lead cases.
\item For JJAS rainfall at Weeks~3--6, FuXi-S2S has the highest ACC in 19 of 20 region--lead cases against IMERG and in 18 of 20
against IMD. For temperature at long leads, ECMWF is the best single model.
\item After Week~2 rainfall skill is low, and the MME is no longer better than climatology in RMSE.
\item Different scores rank the models differently, so no single model is best for every use.
\item Compared with IMD's operational ERPAS system on 101 matched cases from 2023--2024, FuXi-S2S has higher ACC in Weeks~1--4, while
ERPAS is better in October--December.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibrating FuXi-S2S with a small regional model}
Since FuXi-S2S was the best model for long-lead monsoon rainfall, we tried to improve it rather than build a new model. Its 50-member
ensemble is too narrow over India: in Week~1 only 15.6\% of observed values fall inside the 90\% range of the ensemble
(Figure~\ref{fig:skill}c). We trained a small neural network (42,434 parameters), which we call a regional adapter~\cite{raj2026adapter}.
It takes the FuXi-S2S members as input and predicts a shift $\delta$ and a spread factor $s$ for every grid point and lead time. These
are applied to each member after a log transform, $u_m=\log(1+x_m)$:
\begin{equation}
q_m = u_m + \delta + (s-1)\,(u_m-\bar u).
\end{equation}
The model was trained on FuXi-S2S reforecasts from 2002--2017 against IMD rainfall by minimising the CRPS, and the years 2018--2019
were used for model selection. Table~\ref{tab:adapter} gives the results for the 2022--2024 monsoon seasons. The adapter lowers the
CRPS by 16.2\% against IMD and by 12.2\% against IMERG, and the ACC also improves slightly. A separate U-Net model for the ensemble
mean reduced the RMSE by 8.5\% and raised the ACC from 0.276 to 0.364. The coverage is still below 90\%, and so far the method has
been tested for one model and one variable.

\subsection{Combining models with statistical methods}
We also tested whether a learned combination of seven models can do better than their simple average. We used PiggyCast, a
stacking method based on XGBoost~\cite{kimani2025piggycast,chen2016xgboost}, trained on 2020--2023, tuned on 2024 and tested once on
the 2025 monsoon against IMD rainfall~\cite{raj2026piggy}. The ACC increased from 0.307 to 0.347 and the RMSE decreased from 5.174 to
4.989\,mm\,day$^{-1}$. However, a simple bias correction gave a similar result (ACC 0.369), and the gain was only in Weeks~1--4. With
only a few years of data, simple methods are hard to beat, so new methods should always be compared with them.

\subsection{Choice of observations}
We also found that the choice of observed data matters. IMD and IMERG rainfall differ, mostly in regions with heavy rain, and the
improvement from the adapter changed from 16.2\% to 12.2\% between the two. For this reason we verify all results against both.

\subsection{Forecast website}
We have set up an experimental forecast website, \url{https://s2s.scdlds.com}, hosted at SCDLDS. It shows weekly rainfall and 2\,m
temperature forecasts and their anomalies for Weeks~1--6 from a 100-member FuXi-S2S ensemble, and it is updated twice a week. The site
is meant for research and testing, not as an official forecast.

% ============================ 3. PLAN ================================
\section{Plan for the remaining period}

\subsection{Deep learning based post-processing}
So far we have mostly used statistical methods and small neural networks. In the second part of the thesis we will use deep learning
in two ways.

\textit{Global probability model.} For the ECMWF AI Weather Quest~\cite{aiwq2025} we are building a model that converts the FuXi-S2S
ensemble into probabilities of five rainfall categories for days 19--25 and 26--32 over the whole globe, and then corrects these
probabilities with a U-Net/Transformer network (about 150,000 parameters). Early tests against IMERG gave about 3--4\% RPSS over
climatology. The full evaluation against ERA5 is not finished yet.

\textit{Generative model.} The regional adapter can only shift and scale the ensemble. We plan to train a conditional generative model
based on flow matching~\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified} that produces rainfall and temperature fields together, given the
FuXi-S2S forecast $c$. The fields are first compressed by an autoencoder~\cite{rombach2022ldm}, and a network $v_\theta$ learns to move
noise $z_0$ to the observed field $z_1$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{t,z_0,z_1}\big\|\,v_\theta\big((1-t)z_0+t z_1,\,t,\,c\big)-(z_1-z_0)\big\|^2 .
\end{equation}
This should give more realistic ensembles and keep the relation between rainfall and temperature. A similar approach was recently
used for S2S rainfall~\cite{shi2026generative}.

\subsection{Evaluation and timeline}
All new methods will be compared with simple bias correction, EMOS~\cite{gneiting2005emos} and the regional adapter, using CRPS,
reliability and coverage, against both IMD and IMERG. The 2025 monsoon is kept aside as the final test. Table~\ref{tab:plan} gives
the planned timeline.
