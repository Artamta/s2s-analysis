\begin{center}{\normalsize\scshape\spaced{Abstract}}\end{center}
\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{quote}\small\noindent
India has no common yardstick for Weeks~1--6 forecasts, although rain-fed agriculture, reservoir operation and disaster preparedness
depend on how the monsoon behaves a few weeks ahead. In the first half of this thesis we (i)~built IndiaS2S-Bench~\cite{raj2026bench},
a regional benchmark that scores nine operational NWP and AI/hybrid S2S systems on shared dates, regions, seasons, metrics and two
rainfall references; (ii)~showed that FuXi-S2S, the strongest long-lead monsoon model, is strongly underdispersed over India and
corrected it with a 42k-parameter regional probabilistic adapter that lowers rainfall CRPS by 16.2\%~\cite{raj2026adapter};
(iii)~tested classical multi-model post-processing on a frozen 2025 monsoon test, finding that simple calibration is a hard baseline to
beat~\cite{raj2026piggy}; and (iv)~deployed the pipeline as an experimental forecast service (\url{https://s2s.scdlds.com}). The second
half moves from statistical to deep-learning post-processing: a global neural probability adapter and a latent flow-matching generator
of joint rainfall--temperature ensembles.
\end{quote}

% ============================ 1. MOTIVATION ==========================
\section{Introduction}
Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) prediction, two weeks to two months ahead, fills the gap between weather forecasts and seasonal
outlooks~\cite{vitart2017s2s,white2017apps}. It is when a farmer decides when to sow and a reservoir operator how much to release. In India the southwest monsoon (June--September, JJAS) delivers about three
quarters of the annual rainfall and the northeast monsoon (October--December) much of the rain of Tamil Nadu and coastal Andhra
Pradesh~\cite{imd2022lpa,imd_ne}, so a few weeks of a stalled or vigorous monsoon decide the water budget of a year.

The past three years brought a wave of AI weather and climate models (Pangu, GraphCast, GenCast, FuXi-S2S, TianQuan-S2S, NeuralGCM,
FourCastNet~3, DLESyM)~\cite{bi2023pangu,lam2023graphcast,price2025gencast,chen2024fuxis2s,li2026tianquan,kochkov2024neuralgcm,bonev2025fcn3,cresswell2024dlesym}
alongside the operational NWP systems of ECMWF, NCEP, UKMO, CMA and IMD's extended-range ERPAS~\cite{vitart2017s2s,abhilash2014erpas}.
Global benchmarks (WeatherBench~2, ChaosBench) rank them on global averages~\cite{rasp2024wb2,nathaniel2024chaosbench}, but none answers:
\emph{how do these models behave over India, region by region and season by season?}~\cite{malik2024s2s} The thesis asks three questions:
\begin{enumerate}[label={\scshape rq\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item Which forecast system should be trusted for a given target, lead, region and season over India?
\item Can a small regional model correct a fixed global AI ensemble so that its uncertainty becomes reliable?
\item Can deep generative models learn the joint distribution of weekly rainfall and temperature well enough to serve operational,
      probabilistic S2S forecasts?
\end{enumerate}

\textit{Scientific challenges.}
\textit{Rainfall} is intermittent, zero-inflated and skewed, produced by convection far below grid scale, and organised over India by the
monsoon intraseasonal oscillation (active/break spells, BSISO and MJO)~\cite{lee2013bsiso,goswami2005} and by orography; daily grid-scale
rainfall is predictable for only one to two weeks. We therefore forecast \textit{weekly means}, which filter day-to-day noise and keep the
slow MJO/BSISO, ENSO, IOD and land-surface signal. We score \textit{anomalies} from a lead-dependent
climatology, because the seasonal cycle is trivially predictable and removing it also removes each model's drift; ACC measures pattern
skill, RMSE, MAE and the fair CRPS~\cite{ferro2014fair} measure amount and probabilistic quality, and climatology is the baseline every
forecast must beat. Finally, \textit{rainfall and temperature are coupled}: over Indian land in summer wet weeks are cool and dry spells
hot~\cite{trenberth2005}, and both are tied to the 850~hPa monsoon low-level jet~\cite{wang1999index}, which motivates treating the two
variables jointly.

% ============================ 2. WORK DONE ============================
\section{Work Completed}

\subsection{IndiaS2S-Bench: a regional benchmark (RQ1)}
We assembled archived real-time operational forecasts (CMA, ECMWF, NCEP, UKMO) from the S2S database and ran the AI and hybrid models
ourselves from ERA5 initial states (FuXi-S2S, NeuralGCM, FourCastNet~3, DLESyM-v0/v1), each evaluated after its documented training
cut-off~\cite{raj2026bench}. On 517 rainfall and 516 temperature common Monday/Thursday starts in 2020--2024, forecast days 1--42 become six
weekly means on a 1.5$^\circ$ grid over All-India and the four IMD homogeneous regions, for four seasons. Rainfall is verified against
IMERG~V07 and independently against IMD gauges~\cite{huffman2023imerg,pai2014imd}; 2-m temperature against ERA5~\cite{hersbach2020era5};
all differences carry paired moving-block bootstrap intervals.

\textit{Key findings.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textit{Combining models wins early.} An equal-weight six-system mean (MME$_6$) has the highest Week-1 ACC for rainfall (0.622) and
temperature (0.596) (Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}a,b), and the lowest RMSE in 11 of 12 target--lead cells~\cite{raj2026bench}.
\item \textit{FuXi-S2S wins the monsoon at long leads.} For JJAS rainfall at Weeks~3--6 it has the highest ACC in 19/20 region--lead cells
against IMERG and 18/20 against IMD gauges, and the highest All-India rainfall ACC at Week~6 (0.109 vs 0.057 for the MME). ECMWF is the
strongest single system for long-lead temperature.
\item \textit{Long-lead rainfall is still hard.} Beyond Week~2 the MME no longer beats observed climatology on RMSE ($-7.8$\% at Week~6).
\item \textit{Metrics disagree.} Fair CRPS favours other ensembles (NeuralGCM for rainfall Weeks~1--3, ECMWF thereafter, DLESyM-v1 for
temperature), so the benchmark is a \emph{selection map}, not a leaderboard. Against IMD's operational ERPAS on 101 matched 2023--2024
windows, raw FuXi-S2S has higher ACC at Weeks~1--4 and lower RMSE at three of four, though ERPAS is better in OND.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibrating FuXi-S2S with a lightweight regional adapter (RQ2)}
The benchmark points to FuXi-S2S as the best base for monsoon rainfall, so we refine it rather than replace it. Its raw 50-member
ensemble is strongly underdispersed over India: the central 90\% member range contains only 15.6\% of observed Week-1 values
(Fig.~\ref{fig:skill}c). Our permutation-equivariant probabilistic adapter (42,434 parameters) post-processes the fixed ensemble
without retraining the global model~\cite{raj2026adapter}. A shared member encoder and symmetric pooling feed a regional 3-D CNN that
predicts a shift $\delta$ and spread factor $s$ for every grid cell and lead, applied to each log-rainfall member $u_m=\log(1+x_m)$:
\begin{equation}
q_m = u_m + \delta + (s-1)\,(u_m-\bar u), \qquad s>0,
\end{equation}
trained by minimising area-weighted ensemble CRPS against IMD gauge rainfall on 2002--2017 FuXi reforecasts, with the checkpoint chosen on 2018--2019.

A companion deterministic U-Net + cross-lead transformer corrects the ensemble mean, cutting RMSE by 8.5\% and raising ACC from 0.276 to
0.364; on the 2020--2024 cohort the calibrated FuXi mean beats the six-system MME on RMSE at every lead (by 4--10\%), and it beats ERPAS at
all four shared leads on 26 matched cases (with a 24-hour newer start)~\cite{raj2026adapter}. Gains are largest at short leads and in East \&
Northeast India. The limits are equally clear: coverage stays below the nominal 0.90, signed bias is unresolved, and the evidence covers
one model, one variable and three evaluation years; the 2025 season is kept sealed as the first prospective test.

\subsection{Statistical multi-model post-processing}
\label{sec:mm}
Since no single model is best everywhere, we asked whether a learned combination can do better. We aligned seven physics, data-driven and hybrid systems on
one India grid and adapted PiggyCast~\cite{kimani2025piggycast}, a stacking approach with XGBoost~\cite{chen2016xgboost}, to learn
forecast-conditioned weights from JJAS hindcasts, verified against IMD rainfall~\cite{raj2026piggy}. The protocol was \emph{frozen before}
the 2025 monsoon was opened: fitting on 2020--2023, selection on 2024, one confirmatory run on 2025.
The stacker raised mean spatial ACC from 0.307 (equal weights) to 0.347 ($+0.040$, 95\% interval $[0.011, 0.070]$) and lowered RMSE from
5.174 to 4.989~mm\,d$^{-1}$. But the gain is confined to Weeks~1--4, hides a loss in East/Northeast India, does not clear the pre-declared gate
against the best single system, and an exploratory per-lead, per-grid \emph{additive bias correction} alone reaches ACC 0.369. With only a few
years of common hindcasts, simple calibration is therefore a strong baseline, and learned methods must be shown to beat it, not just the
raw ensemble (\S\ref{sec:dl}).

\subsection{Experimental forecast service}
The pipeline is deployed as an experimental forecast service, \url{https://s2s.scdlds.com}, hosted by SCDLDS. It publishes a 100-member FuXi-S2S ensemble twice weekly as Weeks~1--6 maps of weekly rainfall and 2-m
temperature and their anomalies from a lead-matched 2002--2021 reforecast climatology, together with 850~hPa wind anomalies and a
downloadable weekly guidance report. Every issue labels its initialisation source (near-real-time GFS proxy, delayed ERA5 reference, or
an IFS-initialised pilot), and the site is clearly marked as experimental research guidance, not an operational warning. It is the vehicle
through which calibrated and generative forecasts will be tested with users.

\subsection{Observational uncertainty}
The observational reference is itself a source of uncertainty. India's rainfall ``truths'' (IMD gauges~\cite{pai2014imd},
satellite--gauge IMERG~\cite{huffman2023imerg}, CPC, CHIRPS, and ERA5~\cite{hersbach2020era5}, whose rainfall is a model output) differ in gauge
density, retrieval error and day boundaries, most where rain is heaviest~\cite{prakash2018,sun2018review}. In our own work the reference changed the adapter's skill
from 16.2\% (IMD) to 12.2\% (IMERG), changed the long-lead ranking in the benchmark, and in the multi-model study (\S\ref{sec:mm}) even flipped the sign of the
stacker's ACC gain on a matched early-2025 subset. We therefore verify against at least two independent references and treat their spread
as a floor on what can honestly be claimed.

\subsection{A global neural probability adapter}
To test whether a neural corrector generalises beyond India, we built a global adapter for the ECMWF AI Weather Quest~\cite{aiwq2025}:
FuXi-S2S's 51 members are converted into five ERA5 rainfall-quintile probabilities for days 19--25 and 26--32 on the global 1.5$^\circ$
grid, and a compact U-Net/Transformer (151,573 parameters) that sees six forecast weeks of rainfall, moisture and temperature predicts
correction logits around these smoothed raw probabilities. It uses 2,080 FuXi reforecasts (2002--2021), split into train, selection and a
sealed test year. In a small preliminary IMERG experiment, an earlier spatial version gave +2.9\% RPSS over climatology on the selection
slice and +3.7\% in a 2020 retrospective, about 1.9\% beyond calibrated FuXi probabilities, while temporal attention gave no robust gain.
These are engineering evidence only; the full ERA5 evaluation is in progress.

\subsection{Generative calibration with latent flow matching (RQ3)}
The adapter only shifts and stretches members; it cannot reshape the rainfall distribution, represent dry--wet extremes or couple
variables. We will build a conditional generative calibrator based on flow matching~\cite{lipman2023flow,liu2023rectified}.
An autoencoder compresses weekly observed rainfall and temperature fields into a latent $z$~\cite{rombach2022ldm}; a network $v_\theta$ learns
a velocity field that carries Gaussian noise $z_0$ to the observed latent $z_1$, conditioned on the FuXi-S2S ensemble, lead, season and
static context $c$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{t,z_0,z_1}\big\|\,v_\theta\big((1-t)z_0+t z_1,\,t,\,c\big)-(z_1-z_0)\big\|^2 .
\end{equation}
Integrating the learned flow from many noise draws gives a calibrated ensemble of \emph{joint} rainfall--temperature fields that keeps their
physical coupling. Flow matching trains as simply as regression and samples in a few steps, which suits a 42-day, many-member product; it builds
on diffusion ensemble generation~\cite{price2025gencast} and recent generative S2S post-processing~\cite{shi2026generative}. Because data are
scarce, the generator will be pre-trained on the 20-year FuXi reforecast archive and judged against the Level-1 baselines above.

\subsection{Evaluation plan and timeline}
Each method is scored with the frozen IndiaS2S-Bench protocol against additive bias, EMOS and CSGD~\cite{gneiting2005emos,scheuerer2015csgd}
baselines and the adapter (CRPS, reliability, wet/dry-week Brier scores, coverage), against IMD and IMERG, on the sealed 2025 monsoon.

be trusted region by region, verified against more than one observational truth, and delivered to the people who plan around the monsoon.
