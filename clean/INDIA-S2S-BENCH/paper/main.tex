\begin{abstract}
Weekly rainfall guidance at subseasonal leads could support climate-adaptation
planning, but comparisons across forecasting systems are easily confounded by
initialization calendars, valid dates, grids, and climatologies. We align seven
physics, data-driven, and hybrid systems on one 1.5-degree India grid and
evaluate JJAS initializations at lead Weeks 1--6 against India Meteorological
Department (IMD) rainfall. Initial fitting uses 2020--2023; 2024 selects tree
counts; final models refit through 2024; and no 2025 data enter fitting or
selection. A compact
XGBoost adaptation of PiggyCast raises mean case-level spatial anomaly
correlation from \EqualACC{} for the equal-weight mean to \FullACC{}
(paired difference \FullEqualEffect{}, 95\% moving-block interval
[\FullEqualLow{}, \FullEqualHigh{}]) and reduces RMSE from \EqualRMSE{} to
\FullRMSE{} mm day$^{-1}$. The result is deliberately bounded:
the interval against a per-lead 2024-RMSE-selected individual baseline
overlaps zero; ACC gains occur at Weeks 1--4 but not Weeks 5--6; and a
positive national mean hides a clear loss in east/northeast India.
Forecast-conditioned mixing therefore improves a simple ensemble baseline,
but is not uniformly superior. We release case-level scores, coverage,
trained post-processors, uncertainty intervals, and provenance hashes so every
reported numerical claim is auditable.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} subseasonal forecasting; Indian monsoon;
precipitation; multi-model ensemble; post-processing

\section{Introduction}

The two-to-six-week range sits between weather prediction and seasonal
outlooks. Skillful weekly rainfall guidance could inform agricultural
advisories, reservoir preparedness, and flood-readiness decisions, but those
uses require evidence at the relevant lead, season, and region. The
WWRP/WCRP S2S database enabled multi-centre comparison
\citep{vitart2017s2s}; recent AI systems such as FuXi-S2S
\citep{chen2024fuxi}, NeuralGCM \citep{kochkov2024neuralgcm}, and DLESyM
\citep{cresswellclay2024dlesym} add architectural diversity. It remains
unclear whether a small post-processor can exploit these heterogeneous
forecasts for Indian monsoon rainfall without retraining the base systems.

Regional benchmark claims are fragile when forecasts do not share the same
initializations, valid-day endpoints, spatial support, or anomaly reference.
Test-year reuse can further turn an apparent gain into model selection. We
therefore ask: \emph{When does lightweight mixing improve common-grid JJAS
rainfall guidance over India?}

We contribute: (1) a valid-date-aligned benchmark of seven systems for Weeks
1--6 nationally and over four IMD monsoon regions; (2) forecast-only, full,
and location/calendar-only adaptations of PiggyCast
\citep{kimani2025piggycast}, alongside equal-weight, validation-weighted, and
per-lead selected baselines; and (3) a 2025 evaluation with paired
moving-block intervals and explicit claim gates. The useful result is not a
claim of universal superiority: mixing improves equal weighting on average,
while the lead-wise and regional failures identify where such a method should
not be trusted without guardrails.

\section{Benchmark and protocol}

\paragraph{Forecast cohort and valid dates.}
We evaluate weekly ensemble-mean precipitation from CMA, DLESyM v0, ECMWF,
FuXi-S2S, NCEP, NeuralGCM, and UKMO. Each retained initialization has 42
complete daily rates. Week 1 averages accumulation intervals ending at
initialization+1 through +7 days; Week 6 averages +36 through +42. We use the
common initialization intersection: 140 cases from 2020--2023 for fitting, 30
cases in 2024 for every choice, and \TestCases{} cases in 2025 for evaluation.
The exact dates, ensemble sizes, and missing 2024 dates are reported in the
appendix.

\paragraph{Truth, grid, and regions.}
IMD gauge-based daily rainfall \citep{pai2014imd} is conservatively aggregated
to a $27\times27$, 1.5-degree grid. Cell weights combine spherical area,
fractional national or regional overlap, and the observed IMD fraction. We use
India and the northwest, central, south-peninsula, and east/northeast IMD
monsoon regions. A fixed 31-day-window IMD 1991--2019 normal is averaged over
each forecast's exact seven valid dates and subtracted from both forecast and
observation. Thus no model-specific climatology enters ACC. Finite negative
precipitation increments from accumulated-field differencing are retained
rather than selectively clipped.

\paragraph{Pre-specified split.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} summarizes the workflow. Model and feature sets,
hyperparameters, anomaly construction, metrics, and uncertainty procedure
were recorded before scoring 2025 within this workflow. The 2025 values are
not used for normalization, calibration, fitting, early stopping, threshold
choice, feature choice, or retry selection. This is an auditable workflow
property, not a claim that the underlying source archives have never been
viewed for other projects.

\section{Methods}

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The equal-weight mean averages the seven systems. A validation-weighted mean
uses non-negative inverse-2024-RMSE weights separately by lead. A stronger
pre-specified comparator selects the lowest-2024-RMSE individual system at
each lead: FuXi-S2S at Week 1 and ECMWF at Weeks 2--6. We call this the
\emph{per-lead 2024-RMSE-selected baseline}; it is not one fixed model and was
selected by RMSE even though ACC is our primary evaluation metric.

\paragraph{Forecast-conditioned mixing.}
Following PiggyCast, we fit one XGBoost regressor
\citep{chen2016xgboost} per lead. The forecast-only variant uses seven forecast
anomalies and the six available ensemble-spread fields (DLESyM is
deterministic). The full variant adds latitude, longitude, and sine/cosine of
verification-midpoint day of year. A location/calendar-only ablation removes
all forecast inputs. Early stopping on 2024 selects the tree count; a fresh
model with that count is refit using 2020--2024 and applied once to 2025.
Area weights are used in fitting and validation. \emph{Lightweight} here means
six compact tree models and no retraining of any forecasting system; we do not
make an unmeasured compute or data-efficiency claim.

\paragraph{Scores and uncertainty.}
The primary score is case-level, area-weighted spatial anomaly correlation
(ACC), averaged equally across initialization--week cases. Secondary scores
are RMSE, MAE, signed bias, and wet-area fraction error at 1 mm day$^{-1}$.
We form paired percentile intervals from 2,000 circular moving-block draws
over ordered initialization dates, keeping all six leads from an
initialization together. The primary block length is four initializations;
lengths two and eight are sensitivity checks. These are descriptive effect
intervals, not $p$-values.

Full PiggyCast has the highest mean ACC (\FullACC{}) and lowest RMSE

[\FullEqualLow{}, \FullEqualHigh{}], while RMSE changes by

day$^{-1}$. MAE falls from \EqualMAE{} to \FullMAE{}, and signed bias changes
from \EqualBias{} to \FullBias{} mm day$^{-1}$. These jointly rule out an
ACC-only improvement accompanied by worse rainfall-amplitude error.

Simple inverse-validation-RMSE weighting provides only a small point gain
(\WeightedACC{} versus \EqualACC{} ACC). The forecast-only mixer reaches
\ForecastOnlyACC{} ACC and \ForecastOnlyRMSE{} mm day$^{-1}$ RMSE. Its ACC
difference from the location/calendar-only ablation is
\ForecastLocationEffect{} [\ForecastLocationLow{},
\ForecastLocationHigh{}], establishing that forecast-state inputs carry
useful information. This ablation does not, by itself, prove that every model
contributes complementary information.

The stricter comparison remains uncertain. Full PiggyCast exceeds the
per-lead 2024-RMSE-selected baseline by \FullSelectedEffect{} mean ACC, but the
interval [\FullSelectedLow{}, \FullSelectedHigh{}] overlaps zero. Accordingly,
the predeclared headline gate for robust superiority to that baseline is
closed, even though its point ACC, RMSE, and MAE are favorable.

\section{Implications and limitations}

The evidence supports a bounded forecast-quality conclusion: compact,
forecast-conditioned mixing improves an uncalibrated equal-weight ensemble
over India on average, with the clearest value at Weeks 1--4 and in northwest
and south-peninsula regions. It does not establish uniformly better S2S
prediction, operational value, or improved decisions. A cautious deployment
would retain the original forecasts, monitor each lead and region, and abstain
where validation does not support the mixer.

The evaluation contains only \TestCases{} initializations from one year, and
serial dependence limits precision. The full-versus-selected lower interval
endpoint changes from \SelectedBlockTwoLow{} at block length two to
\SelectedBlockFourLow{} and \SelectedBlockEightLow{} at lengths four and
eight; the equal-weight conclusion remains positive at all three lengths.
The benchmark evaluates deterministic ensemble means, not probabilistic
calibration, extremes, monsoon onset, or decision value. Ensemble sizes and
model versions differ, and five common 2024 dates are missing.

IMD is the sole observational reference in the reported results, so gauge
coverage, one 1991--2019 normal, and nonstationarity remain sources of
uncertainty. Approximately \FullNegativePercent{}\% of full-PiggyCast's
area-weighted national support is negative under the pre-specified no-clipping
policy;
physical non-negativity should be handled in a future calibration study, not
silently repaired after seeing test scores. Finally, a planned FuXi neural
correction is excluded because its saved evaluator defines Week 1 as
initialization+0--6 rather than the benchmark's +1--7 endpoints. Comparing it
would trade methodological variety for a one-day label error.

\paragraph{Climate relevance and responsible use.}
Rainfall guidance can affect risk-sensitive decisions and resource allocation.
The regional failure is therefore not a cosmetic subgroup result: it argues
against presenting one national skill number as readiness for use across
India. This study supplies forecast-quality evidence only. Any downstream
application requires locally relevant thresholds, probabilistic reliability,
cost--loss or impact evaluation, and communication of uncertainty.

\section{Conclusion}

On a common JJAS 2025 benchmark, lightweight PiggyCast mixing improves
equal-weight multi-model rainfall forecasts in national ACC, RMSE, and MAE.
The same experiment also supplies the boundary of that result: uncertainty
against a per-lead selected baseline, no ACC gain at Weeks 5--6, and a clear
east/northeast loss. The central lesson is therefore conditional rather than
promotional: forecast-conditioned mixing can add value, but lead- and
region-specific evaluation must determine where it is used.
